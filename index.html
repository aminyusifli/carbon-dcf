Carbon-Adjusted
Valuation Model

A discounted cash flow model for ArcelorMittal in which the Europe segment's carbon cost is flexed by EUA price and cost pass-through — quantifying what the market must assume about carbon to justify the current share price.

Why this model exists: EUA prices are liquid and publicly traded, and CBAM's phase-out schedule has been public regulation since 2023. A model claiming to have found a mispricing professional analysts missed would not be credible. This one asks a different question: reverse-engineering what carbon cost path ArcelorMittal's current price implies, and how sensitive that valuation is to a tougher path. The dominant variable turns out not to be the carbon price at all — it is whether the cost can be passed on to customers.

Methodology & sources
Model structure
Consolidated DCF with explicit forecast years 2026–2034 — the window over which EU ETS free allocation phases out — then a Gordon Growth terminal value from 2035. Consolidated rather than Europe-only because value per share must be comparable to the traded share price, which reflects the whole company; carbon costs are applied only to the Europe segment. Nine explicit years are necessary because a single-period perpetuity cannot hold a cost that changes every year. Gordon Growth is used over an exit multiple as it is easier to defend line by line and avoids peer-set circularity.
Carbon cost mechanism
Free allocation under EU ETS is benchmark-based, not a percentage of a producer's own emissions: installations receive allowances against an EU efficiency benchmark, and anyone emitting above it pays for the excess from day one. Chargeable intensity = max(0, emissions intensity − benchmark × share still allocated free). Benchmark set at 1.30 tCO₂e/t; the hot metal benchmark alone is 1.248 (Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1412), with separate allocations for coke and sintered ore. Alternative benchmark values were tested and move value by under 3%. Phase-out schedule per EU Regulation 2023/956: 2.5% removed in 2026, rising to 100% by 2034.
Emissions intensity
1.63 tCO₂e per tonne of crude steel — ArcelorMittal's own reported Europe segment figure, Scope 1+2 (20-F sustainability KPI table, FY2025). Cross-checks against absolute disclosure: 47.2 Mt CO₂e ÷ 29.166 Mt production = 1.62. Declines to 1.519 by 2031 as Gijon (1.1Mt, 2H 2026) and Dunkerque (2.0Mt, ramp 2030) electric arc furnace capacity replaces blast-furnace output at roughly 0.60 tCO₂e/t. That path is corroborated by the company's stated 2.85 Mt CO₂ reduction from Dunkirk by 2030 (the model assumes 2.06 Mt — conservative) and its expectation of up to a 10% group intensity reduction by 2030 against 2018.
EUA price paths
Three independent sources, shown separately because they diverge materially and that divergence is itself informative. ECB: March 2026 staff projection technical assumptions (€72.9/t 2026 rising to €75.5/t 2028), extrapolated thereafter. ArcelorMittal internal: the company's own impairment-testing assumptions (€77/t 2026 to €100/t 2030, 20-F p.223) — notably above the market-derived path. Fastmarkets: sector forecast implying ETS costs reaching roughly 34% of blast-furnace production cost by 2035.
Business inputs
EBITDA base $7,486m — H1 2026 actual of $3,743m annualised (Q2 2026 earnings release, 29 July 2026). Plus $1.6bn of strategic project EBITDA phasing in over five years: management guides $1.8bn less the $0.2bn Liberia contribution already captured in 2025, struck against 2015–2020 spreads. D&A $2,945m, consistent with FY2026 guidance of ~$3.0bn. Capex declines linearly from the $4.75bn guidance midpoint toward D&A by 2031 as the decarbonisation investment cycle completes. Associates contribute $324m of cash dividends actually received (20-F cash flow statement, three-year average) rather than $923m of equity-method earnings, which are not cash. Net debt $9,500m and 761.1m shares outstanding.
Discount rate
WACC 10.87%, market-value weighted. Cost of equity 12.52% via CAPM: risk-free 5.17% (US 10-year Treasury, 25 September 2026 — a 19-year high), equity risk premium 4.20% (Damodaran, Luxembourg, Aaa rating carrying zero country risk premium), beta 1.75 (Yahoo Finance 5-year monthly, consistent across the Amsterdam, Madrid and Vienna listings; Investing.com reports 1.68). Cost of debt 5.4% — the weighted average coupon across thirteen outstanding bonds (20-F p.233) rather than an assumed spread. Normalised tax rate 19.5%, derived from the statutory-rate reconciliation (20-F p.281) because reported effective rates swing between 10.0% and 52.7% on disclosed one-offs. Terminal growth 3.0% nominal: roughly 1.0% real euro area potential growth (European Commission 2024 Ageing Report) plus the ECB's 2% inflation target. Cross-check: management uses a 10.7% pre-tax discount rate for Europe impairment testing.
Validation
The no-carbon baseline lands within roughly 13% of the market price using only independently sourced inputs, with nothing reverse-fitted to match — beta in particular was verified against external sources rather than solved for. Terminal value accounts for 47–57% of enterprise value, well below the 70–80% typical of a DCF, meaning the explicit forecast carries more of the valuation. Negative equity values are floored at zero: shareholders have limited liability, so a share cannot be worth less than nothing; a negative result means enterprise value has fallen below net debt and the equity is exhausted.
Limitations
Intensity is Scope 1+2 as reported; EU ETS charges Scope 1 only, but ArcelorMittal does not disclose a Scope 1 split anywhere, so this overstates chargeable emissions. The forecast contains no cyclical downturn — nine consecutive years of growth for a cyclical producer whose FY2021 EBITDA was $19.4bn against FY2025 underlying of $4.9bn. Modelling a cycle would require arbitrary choices about timing and depth, and would not change the central finding, since a downturn affects every scenario equally. Working capital is assumed neutral; Europe production is held flat while group EBITDA grows, which understates carbon cost; the share count is held flat despite ongoing buybacks, which understates value per share; and the risk-free rate is spot at a 19-year high, where a normalised rate would raise every figure. A student research model built on public data without management access. Not investment advice.
